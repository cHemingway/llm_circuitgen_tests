# Solartron 7075 opto-isolated USB interface (RP2354A)

KiCad 10 project for a small board that plugs straight onto the 50-way D
connector of a Solartron 7075 DVM and gives full remote control / read-out over
USB.  An RP2354A talks to the multimeter through shift registers; only six
signals cross the isolation barrier, all through optocouplers.

## What it connects to (please read)

Section 9 of the service manual documents the **70754 Parallel BCD Interface
Unit** and gives the pin-out of its 50-way "Systems Interface" socket **SKB**
(page 9.9).  This board is designed for that connector: J1 pin numbers are SKB
pin numbers and the logic levels are those of page 9.1.

It is *not* designed for the bare DVM rear socket SK1 (the one the 70754 plugs
into).  SK1 carries a different, multiplexed word-serial bus that the manual
only shows on the 70754 circuit diagrams, so a 7075 without the 70754 unit
would need a different board.

## How it works

```
USB-B -> RP2354A -> 74LVC125 -> 3 x HCPL-2631 -> 2 x TPIC6C595 (16 open-drain outputs)  -> SKB 38..50
                                 (6 channels)  <- 5 x 74HCT165  (40 inputs, 100k series) <- SKB 1..36, 38
USB 5 V -> ferrite -> B0509S isolated DC-DC (9 V) -> L78M05 -> +5V_ISO
```

Signals across the barrier:

| Signal | RP2354A | Direction | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCK | GPIO2 (SPI0 SCK) | to DVM side | shift clock for both chains, rising edge shifts |
| MOSI | GPIO3 (SPI0 TX) | to DVM side | command bits into the TPIC6C595 chain |
| MISO | GPIO4 (SPI0 RX) | to MCU | data out of the 74HCT165 chain |
| LATCH | GPIO5 | to DVM side | low = load the 40 inputs; rising edge = copy command word to the outputs |
| OEn | GPIO6 | to DVM side | low = command outputs enabled |
| DRDY | GPIO7 | to MCU | PRINT command level (SKB 34): high = reading complete |

Other pins: GPIO0 / GPIO1 = UART0 TX / RX on test pads TP1 / TP2, GPIO25 =
activity LED, SWD on J3 (Raspberry Pi 3-pin debug connector), BOOTSEL and RESET
buttons.

All six channels are non-inverting end to end and "LED off" is the high level.
While the MCU is in reset or unprogrammed (pull-ups RN1), or USB is unplugged,
OEn is high and every command line is released, so the multimeter behaves
exactly as if nothing were plugged into SKB (front panel stays usable).

### Transfer

1. Pulse LATCH low (>= 1 us).  The falling edge loads SKB pins into the
   74HCT165s; the rising edge copies the last 16 bits previously shifted into
   the TPIC6C595s to the outputs.
2. Clock 40 bits, SPI mode 3 (or 0), <= 1 MHz: send 24 padding bits followed
   by the 16-bit command word MSB first, and read 40 status bits.
3. Pulse LATCH again (or simply start the next transfer) to apply the command.
4. Drive OEn low once the first command word has been latched.

**Status stream** (first bit first): bit n = SKB pin n+1 for n = 0..35
(BCD digits 10^6..10^0, polarity, function, range, PRINT pulse/level, DATA CAN
CHANGE, OVERLOAD).  Bit 36 reads back SKB 38 (FRONT PANEL LOCKOUT line: it is
pulled up by the multimeter, so with the output released a 1 means "DVM
connected and powered").  Bits 37..39 are hard-wired 1, 0, 0 as a check that
the isolated supply and the whole chain are alive.

**Command word**: bit k drives SKB pin 38+k (k = 0..12): 38 front panel
lockout, 39 SAMPLE contact, 40 SAMPLE pulse, 41 ratio, 42/43 function,
44/45/46 integration time (4)(2)(1), 47 autorange, 48/49/50 range (1)(2)(4).
A 1 switches the open-drain output on, i.e. puts logic 0 on the line.  Bit 2
is the exception: a 1 drives SKB 40 *high* through Q1 (the manual asks for a
positive pulse of 3..8 V, > 100 us).  Bits 13..15 are spare outputs.

In REP (free-running) mode the 70754 holds both PRINT outputs low and its
output shift registers ripple while a new reading is transferred, so firmware
should read twice and compare; in single-sample mode use DRDY.

## Design notes

* **Isolation.**  HCPL-2631 dual 10 MBd optocouplers (gull-wing SMD-8) straddle
  a 6.8 mm copper-free strip on all four layers.  The only narrower point is
  between pins 2 and 3 of the SIP-4 DC-DC converter (1.0 mm copper gap, part
  rated 1.5 kV); the project footprint for PS1 uses narrower pads than KiCad's
  stock SIP-4 footprint (0.29 mm gap).  This is functional isolation to break
  the USB ground loop, not a safety barrier.  A custom DRC rule
  (`.kicad_dru`) enforces >= 1 mm between the two domains.
* **Isolated supply.**  SKB has no supply pin, so the DVM-side logic is powered
  from USB through a 1 W isolated converter.  A 9 V converter followed by a
  linear L78M05 is used instead of an unregulated 5 V -> 5 V module, whose
  output would exceed 5.5 V at this light load.  Load is about 65 mA.
* **Inputs.**  The 70754 drives SKB from 7404 TTL outputs, hence 74HCT165 (TTL
  thresholds).  Each line has 100k in series and 100k to GND_ISO: at most
  ~35 uA per pin flows into the unpowered board, and the inputs are defined
  when the board is not plugged in.
* **Outputs.**  TPIC6C595 open-drain shift registers sink the multimeter's own
  4.7k pull-ups (the 70754 inputs are TTL with pull-ups to +5 V) and are
  high-impedance without power.
* **RP2354A.**  Raspberry Pi reference circuit: 12 MHz ABM8-272-T3 crystal,
  polarised AOTA-B201610S3R3 inductor with the VIN/output capacitors stacked
  above pins 47-49 and the switch node routed between their pads as in the
  reference layout.  DVDD pins 6/23 and VREG_FB reach +1V1 through vias under
  the package.
* **Mechanics.**  J1 (DD-50 plug, straight PCB pins, plain 3.1 mm flange holes)
  is the only part on the bottom side; everything else, including the vertical
  USB-B socket, is on the top side.  The board is held by two 4-40 UNC screws
  that pass through the 3.2 mm plated holes (61.11 mm apart) and J1's flange
  into the jackposts of SKB.  The screw-head pads are 6.4 mm and tied to
  GND_ISO through R24 (0R, remove to float the shell).  Screws of about 16 mm
  (5/8") are needed; 5 mm spacers between PCB and flange stop the board
  bending when they are tightened.  Seen from the top with J1's row 1..17
  uppermost, pin 1 is top right and the USB end is on the right.
* **Layers.**  F.Cu / B.Cu signals, In1 split ground plane (GND_ISO | GND),
  In2 split supply plane (+5V_ISO | +3V3).  Rules: 0.2 mm tracks, 0.15 mm
  clearance, 0.6 / 0.3 mm vias (0.15 mm / 0.12 mm only for the regulator switch
  node).

## Parts

Symbols and footprints come from KiCad's own libraries wherever they exist;
every part has `LCSC`, `MPN` and `Manufacturer` fields (LCSC numbers were
checked against the catalogue on 2026-11-11).  Three things are not in the
stock libraries and live in `lib/solartron7075.*`: the DD-50 connector
(symbol + footprint, from Amphenol drawing C-DSUB-0065), the TPIC6C595 symbol
and the SIP-4 DC-DC footprint, plus GND_ISO / +5V_ISO / +9V_ISO power symbols.

Main parts: RP2354A (C41378174), HCPL-2631-500E (C188742), 74HCT165D
(C456131), TPIC6C595DR (C350572), SN74LVC125APWR (C7813), SN74AHCT125DR
(C155176), B0509S-1WR3 (C5369463), L78M05ABDT (C58069), AP2112K-3.3 (C51118),
USBLC6-2SC6 (C7519), TE 5787834-1 USB-B (C592900), Amphenol DD50P364TXLF
(C5402574).  Full list: `production/bom_jlcpcb.csv`.  J1, J2 and PS1 are
through-hole; J1 goes on the opposite side to everything else.

## Verification status

* ERC: no errors.  Two "symbol doesn't match library" warnings (U1, Y1) are a
  KiCad quirk with symbols that have stacked hidden pins; they also appear on a
  file re-saved by KiCad itself.
* DRC: no errors, 0 unconnected, 0 schematic-parity issues; two cosmetic
  silkscreen warnings (J1 outline crossing its screw pads).  Reports are in
  `production/`.
* The board was placed by script and routed by the grid router in
  `scripts/router.py` (no interactive routing), so the tracks are electrically
  clean but not hand-optimised.  Nothing has been built or tested.

Things worth checking before ordering:

1. That your unit has the 70754 fitted and that SKB's jackposts are 4-40 UNC.
2. Which way up SKB is mounted, and that a 116 x 50 mm board centred 36 mm from
   its left edge on the connector clears whatever is next to it.
3. The manual contradicts itself on the FUNCTION command pins: the text on
   page 9.2 has pin 43 low = AC and pin 42 low = ohms, the table on page 9.9
   says the opposite.  The hardware does not care (nets `DVM_FN_CMD_A/B`);
   settle it in firmware.
4. KiCad has no 3D models for J1 (DD-50) and J2 (vertical USB-B), so both are
   missing from the renders; PS1 borrows the model of a pin-compatible Murata SIP-4.

## Rebuilding

```bash
scripts/build.sh
```

needs KiCad 10 (`kicad-cli` and the `pcbnew` Python module).  `gen_lib.py`
writes the project libraries, `gen_sch.py` the schematic, `gen_pcb.py` places
and routes the board, `export.py` writes `docs/` and `production/`.
